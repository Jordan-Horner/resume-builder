## Design Context

### Users

The primary user is an individual job seeker managing a private, local career
workspace. They need to search and filter jobs awaiting a decision, see which jobs they
applied for, follow application status, and configure optional connections
without navigating CLI output or implementation details. The main "aha" moment
is opening the product and immediately working through a small inbox of new
opportunities.

The same user also reviews evidence-grounded resumes before approving a final
PDF. Career professionals may review a shared web preview, while recruiters and
hiring managers receive the minted resume. The dashboard is an operational
workspace; the resume preview is a focused editorial review surface.

### Brand Personality

Credible, calm, and professional. The interface should feel like a carefully
organized editorial operations desk: confident without hype, polished without
decoration, and explicit about freshness, connection health, decisions, and
approval state. It should reduce job-search anxiety by making state and next
actions legible without pretending to predict hiring outcomes.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined editorial-workspace aesthetic based on GitHub Dark Default:
neutral blue-charcoal surfaces, restrained blue accents, soft cool-white text,
semantic status color, strong typography, and clear information density. The job inventory should feel closer to a
well-edited briefing than a generic SaaS card grid: rows carry the work, filters
are compact, and a stable detail pane preserves context. Depth comes from
subtly lighter surface tones and fine borders rather than glowing accents or
heavy shadows. Use blue for selection and navigation, green for positive
actions, amber for attention, and red only for destructive or failed states.

For resume preview, preserve an ATS-safe, single-column paper document with
restrained color and clear hierarchy. It should remain a faithful review surface
for the printable resume rather than inheriting the dashboard layout. Avoid
generic AI styling, decorative cards, visual noise, dark neon themes, fake
metrics, and interface elements that compete with the user's decisions.

### Design Principles

1. Keep the main Jobs page limited to opportunities awaiting disposition and make search,
   work-mode filtering, and the next useful action immediately clear.
2. Preserve the complete inventory: filters may organize jobs but must never
   imply that hidden opportunities ceased to exist.
3. Use compact rows, typography, spacing, and alignment instead of a card-heavy
   dashboard.
4. Keep integration setup short: explain the data boundary, collect only what
   is required, verify the connection, and show how to recover from failure.
5. Keep essential search, filters, status, and setup actions available across
   screen sizes.
6. Keep the resume itself as the focus of resume-review screens and protect
   print integrity from screen-only styling.
7. Use accessible status signals and verify keyboard operation, zoom, long
   content, empty results, and failures.
